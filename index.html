        How ordering works

        LEGO Pick a Brick is LEGO’s own shop for single pieces. “Buy on LEGO Pick a Brick” downloads your list (). On Pick a Brick, click “Upload List”, choose the file, then check your bag: LEGO lists any piece it couldn’t add. Uploading a list isn’t available in every country: check Pick a Brick for yours.

        BrickLink is a marketplace of independent shops selling single pieces, including the ones LEGO has out of stock. “Buy on BrickLink” copies your list. Sign in (with a LEGO account), open Want → Upload, choose “Upload BrickLink XML format”, paste, then open the new wanted list and use “Buy All” to find shops.

        The cost shown is an estimate from Pick a Brick prices (Sept 2026), an order of magnitude only: your shop sets the real price, plus shipping.

        Questions

        Is this official?

        It is a free, open-source tool from the riggles project, and it is not affiliated with, sponsored or endorsed by the LEGO Group or BrickLink. It sells nothing and earns nothing: no affiliate links, no commission, no tracking. Build your own riggle, for personal use: please don’t sell the models, the instructions or kits made from them.

        Where does the riggle come from?

        From the chain, by way of its hash. Every riggle is drawn by one engine from one 32 byte hash fixed at mint, and that engine is in this page. Type a number and the page looks up that riggle’s hash, hands it to the engine, and reads the square wave back out: the runs, the risers and the colour at every point along them. That line is what the bricks follow. Nothing is fetched while you use the site and no key is needed, because the hashes were read from the riggles API once, when the site was built.

        Can I really build it?

        Every model is checked by computer on its final piece list: all pieces are standard parts in real colours, every piece is attached (0 floating), no two pieces overlap (0 collisions), every piece of the riggle is held by at least two studs, and the table displays keep their centre of mass over the base. The site shows these checks for your model and says so plainly if one fails. Models have not been built with real bricks yet, so treat your first build as a test.

        Relief or Mosaic? Frame, Floating or Backdrop?

        The Relief turns the riggle into a ribbon of bricks that steps up where the wave passes over itself, as the drawing does. Hang it in its black Frame, stand it above a black base on clear supports (Floating), or in front of a white brick wall (Backdrop): the ribbon is the same in all three. The Mosaic is flat, tiles on black plates. A riggle suits bricks either way, because it is already made of right angles: every run lands along a row of studs and every corner is square.

        What are the clear pieces?

        On Floating and Backdrop, the ribbon stands on small Trans-Clear round pieces: a round 1×1 plate in a hole of the base, and two round 1×1 bricks on it. They are the hidden supports, and almost disappear. The instructions show where each one goes.

        How do I hang the Frame?

        Behind the Frame are four black Technic bricks with holes. Tie a picture cord through the holes of the two at the top and hang it on a picture hook, or on two hooks or a screw with a wall plug for the heavier Large Frame: the site tells you which for your model. The two bottom bricks keep it flat against the wall. The instructions have a “Hanging” page.

        How do I get the bricks? What does it cost?

        Under “Buy the bricks”: download the Pick a Brick file and upload it on LEGO Pick a Brick (“Upload List”), or copy the BrickLink list and upload it on BrickLink (Want → Upload). Every part of these models has a LEGO Element ID (stock varies). The cost shown is an estimate from Pick a Brick prices, an order of magnitude: you order and pay on those sites, never here.
